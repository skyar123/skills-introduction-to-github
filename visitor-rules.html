VISITOR RULES FOR REMI & SCOUT

(please help us keep them safe)

Why this matters: Our twins were born 10 weeks early (April 10th, due June 21st) and are NICU graduates. Even though they're home now, their lungs, immune system, and other organs are still catching up developmentally. Preemies face higher risks from respiratory infections and illnesses that might be mild for full-term babies.

🌡️

1. ARE YOU WELL & VACCINATED?

  • If you have any symptoms (cough, runny nose, fever, sore throat, stomach bug, "just allergies" that make you sneeze/cough), don't visit. Reschedule.
  • Tdap (whooping cough), flu, and COVID shots should be up to date (≥2 weeks before visiting). Babies under 6 months can't get flu vaccine; they rely on you.

(RSV note: Ask us if we've done the RSV antibody shot (nirsevimab). Even with it, we still limit exposures.)

💋

2. COLD SORES = NO KISSES

  • If you've had a recent cold sore or even "tingling," no kissing, no holding.
  • No one kisses the babies' faces or hands—ever. Parents included if a sore is active. HSV can be fatal in young infants.
🧼

3. NICU-LEVEL HAND HYGIENE

  • Wash hands with soap for 20 seconds (rings off), or sanitize before touching. Limit jewelry that traps germs.
  • Don't touch their faces or hands (hands go straight to mouth).
😷

4. MASK IF UNSURE

  • If you work around kids, were sick recently, or it's virus season, wear a mask and keep distance from their faces. Masking reduces droplet spread.
⏱️

5. SHORT & SMALL VISITS

  • Keep visits brief and limit the number of people at once. Fewer exposures = less risk.
🚭

6. NO SMOKE / VAPE RESIDUE

  • No smoking or vaping here. If you smoke, arrive in freshly laundered clothes and clean hands. Thirdhand smoke chemicals stick to fabric/hair and are toxic to infants.
🚫

7. FRAGRANCE-FREE ZONE

  • Skip perfume, cologne, aftershave, scented lotions, strong hair products, and scented laundry products. Volatile compounds and phthalates irritate fragile lungs and skin.
⚠️

WHAT TO WATCH FOR (If you're here and notice this, tell us)

  • Breathing trouble: fast, labored, wheezing, grunting; nostril flaring; chest retractions.
  • Color changes: pale, blue/gray lips or nails.
  • Behavior shifts: extreme sleepiness or irritability, poor feeding, vomiting.

Call our pediatrician/ER if these show up—fast matters.

Thanks for protecting their tiny lungs & immune systems. Questions? Ask us—no offense taken.
